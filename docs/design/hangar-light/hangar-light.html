The contact sheet. Every hull at every angle worth judging it from, drawn once and held still. Turning one ship at a time and remembering what the last one looked like is how you end up with five hulls that are each defensible and do not look like a fleet — a deck laid down the centreline of a ship that is not straight, a prow whose trenches have been bridged over, the same nose cap on all of them. In the sheet those are visible at a glance. Plan view is the column to trust: it is the only one the sprite can vouch for.

These are the real hulls. Starsector is drawn top-down, so a ship's plan view is already on screen — and the silhouettes here are marched off the alpha channel of the installed sprite, not off the collision polygon in data/hulls. The bounds are what the game hit-tests: they inscribe the artwork and chord across every sweep. The Odyssey's flanks are scalloped by three circular pods a side and the bounds render that as one straight line. Canvas, no 3D library, no external asset. Drag to turn it.

Each contour is a thousand-odd pixels reduced to around 110. Rebuilding the far flank from the near one was tried and is worse than useless: it joins the two ends of the walk straight across whatever sits between them, which welded the Conquest's bow channel shut into a spire. The whole loop is simplified instead, finely enough that the flanks do not argue.

The Odyssey is not folded. Four of these five hulls are drawn symmetric to within 1.6–4.5 % of their pixels — rendering noise — so their masks are folded about their own centreline before tracing, which costs nothing and removes the wobble. The Odyssey's figure is 16.4 %: it is asymmetric on purpose. Folding it is not a fix, it deletes the thing that makes the ship recognisable, so it is traced verbatim.

The deck is written down, not worked out. Five stations per hull, read off its own sprite — the nose cap, the pod rows or the broadsides, the bridge, the face of the engine block — each carrying how high the deck stands there and how wide its flat top is. Width is what stops it looking like a ladder: a station with no width is a knife ridge, a wide one is a plate, and where one is wider than the station before it the deck overhangs, where narrower it slopes in. The tops are joined down the length of the ship, so the superstructure is drawn by two long edges rather than a rung at every station.

Evenly spaced ribs were what read as ruled. Scoring the artwork to choose better stations automatically was tried next and was barely different — it finds the rows that are busy, not the rows that are structure. Five ships this iconic are worth reading by hand.

The holes are free now. A Paragon is a donut and a Conquest has a slot down its middle. The bounds encode those as a zero-width seam walked in and back out, which every earlier version either sealed shut or tore the hull open on. A traced void is just another closed loop, so it needs no special case: it is drawn like any other ring, the even-odd test reads it as outside, and no rib or ridge segment crosses it.

Still an interpretation: the third axis is invented — deck height, keel, ridge, where the plate thins into an edge. None of that exists in a flat sprite. The silhouette is the only part that can be faithful, which is why it is now exact.

Three ways to say eleven hours

The number carries differently depending on how much furniture it gets. All three show the same ledger; the difference is what the reader is invited to do next.

You’ve played 9.6 hours of Starsector through Preflight, across 98 sessions since July.

Friendliest, and the first one to get annoying — it says the same sentence every time you open the app, and it can’t be scanned.

Why the filter has to be visible

Sub-minute launches are 71 % of the sessions and 14 % of the hours

Your ledger is mostly benchmark runs. That is what makes a hidden threshold dangerous: whichever number Preflight prints, it is throwing away a lot of something. So it prints the rule next to the total instead of applying it silently.

The palette

Warm grey, gold pushed toward orange

Deus Ex gold sits around #d4af37 — yellow-green and heavily saturated. These are rotated about 12° toward orange and pulled down in saturation, so gold reads as a material rather than a highlighter. The neutrals carry the same hue bias, which is what stops them looking like default grey.

Preflight ships light and dark. The gold survives both, which is the actual test — a saturated gold on warm paper turns to mustard. Use the invert button above to check.

Standing answers

“For Starsector” is gone entirely

The wordmark is the mark plus Preflight, nothing else. The only things naming the game are the launch button and the install path — so the window says what it is, but the brand doesn’t. Put it back as a subtitle on the sidebar mark only, at label size.

Verdict · restore it, one line, sidebar only

The funding area is already small enough — the problem is that it’s three

Three platform names in a row read as a campaign; one reads as a tip jar. Only buymeacoffee.com/teamleaderleo currently resolves — Ko-fi and Patreon return 403 to a scripted request, which is bot protection rather than proof either way, so you’d need to confirm those handles are actually claimed.

Needs you · which handles exist? Ship only those.

The wireframe hull earns its place by replacing dead space

Home currently has an empty lower half. The hull is not decoration added to a full page — it is the idle state of a page that already had a hole in it, and it gives the hour count somewhere to live.

Verdict · prototype above, yours to kill

Pick the hull the way you'd pick a mascot

Five are wired up, and the Odyssey is now the default — it is the one that most rewards being made three-dimensional, because a long dart with swept aft wings reads as motion from almost any angle. The Onslaught is the louder pick and the more obvious mascot. Conquest is the prettiest and the worst choice: long and thin, so half its rotation is a sliver. Hammerhead is the sentimental one.

Needs you · Odyssey by default, Onslaught if you want volume
